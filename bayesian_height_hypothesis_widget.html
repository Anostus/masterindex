Bayesian Height Hypothesis Playground

A toy model for hypothetical Populations A, B, and C. Heights are expressed as z-scores relative to Population C, whose observed and genetic mean is set to 0. This is for sensitivity analysis only, not for drawing conclusions about real human groups.

H1: A genetic mean = C; A short due to environment H2: A and B have below-C latent genetic height means B = founder source from A + C admixture + uncertainty

Model sketch

Observed z = genetic z − environmental penalty + error

B genetic z = (1 − admixture) × A-founder source + admixture × C-source

Posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio

H2 marginalizes over an uncertain A genetic shortfall rather than only using its best-fitting value.

What the sliders mean

Founder percentile: converted into a normal z-score and scaled by subpopulation genetic spread and phenotype-to-genetic conversion. Example: a 20th percentile source has a negative founder offset.

Admixture: B's genetic mean is a weighted average of the founder source from A and the C-contributor source.

Environment: subtracts from genetic potential to generate observed height. Raising A's environmental penalty tends to help H1; lowering it tends to help H2.

Uncertainty: larger uncertainty makes the posterior less extreme. Small uncertainty values can make this toy model overconfident.

Important limitation: a real analysis would need representative genotype data, measured childhood environments, sex and age stratification, migration/admixture history, uncertainty in phenotypic SDs, and explicit causal assumptions. This widget deliberately avoids claims about real populations.
